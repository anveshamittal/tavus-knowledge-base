## Customer Case Study

### A Century-Old Company, Newly Independent
Carrier is a leading provider of healthy, safe, intelligent, and sustainable building and cold chain solutions. It has been around for more than a century and, at the time of the video, was in its second year as an independent company. Carrier operates in three business segments: HVAC, refrigeration, and fire, security and safety, and it has applied technology across many of its brands. During the pandemic, Carrier's cold chain technology helped safely distribute COVID-19 vaccines, keeping them at the prescribed sub-zero temperatures. The company does all of this with a global workforce of about 56,000 employees serving customers in more than 160 countries.

### Moving Beyond Hub-and-Spoke and VPNs
Carrier ran a very traditional hub-and-spoke architecture built around heavy data centers, backhauling most of its traffic through them. Security followed a castle-and-moat model based on firewalls, with a heavy reliance on virtual private networks for remote access. That approach was costly and very complex to operate. Carrier wanted a modern, cloud-native platform with more local internet breakouts, so every endpoint would be fully serviced with the right level of performance.

### Core Principles: Cloud, SaaS, Zero Trust and Identity
Carrier anchored its program on core principles: being cloud-native, embracing cloud and SaaS so the business can pivot quickly, and leveraging zero trust. In cybersecurity, the key focus areas were identity and access management, with the program built on least-privilege access so users only have the access they need. Zscaler allowed Carrier to deploy a security stack very quickly, giving people secure access anytime and anywhere while checking the security posture of their devices.

### A Nine-Day Rollout Before the Pandemic
Carrier made a big bet on Zscaler as its platform of choice and rolled it out in nine days, right before COVID-19 sent the workforce home. The deployment was a cross-functional effort across the infrastructure, application, cybersecurity and business teams. Carrier set up a war room, deployed the solution to key stakeholders first, and tested extensively to make sure their applications worked over Zscaler Internet Access (ZIA) and Zscaler Private Access (ZPA). Users could reach the applications they absolutely needed without being placed on the network, which kept everyone safe, including employees who had not traditionally worked remotely. Carrier retired its VPNs quickly and, within nine days, had transitioned everybody to its zero trust infrastructure.

### Simplifying Mergers, Acquisitions and Divestitures
Zscaler has solved many M&A problems for Carrier. Because users connect to applications rather than the network, Carrier no longer has to put new users on its network when it acquires or divests an organization, so it does not introduce new vulnerabilities into its environment. Previously, Carrier carried out thorough hygiene evaluations of organizations it planned to buy or divest, followed by a lengthy process to onboard them. That is no longer necessary. Carrier's risk posture has dramatically improved, the time to onboard is down to hours, far less up-front planning is required, and the cost of an acquisition or divestiture has fallen.

### Positioned for the Next Few Decades
Nicole Darden Ford describes the result as monumental: a better digital program for Carrier that has positioned the company for the next few decades.
