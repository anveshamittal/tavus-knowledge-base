## Customer Case Study
ADK Holdings Inc., a full-service advertising company built around communication, has taken on a renewal of its IT infrastructure. The trigger was the ransomware damage that kept hitting companies close to home. For a company that handles clients' confidential information within the group, it was a danger that could not be ignored. Site-by-site security also raised risk. Every M&A deal or new site meant installing another firewall, and a shortage of staff delayed rollouts and left operations and management reactive. ADK decided to adopt Zscaler, which can be managed centrally from the cloud. In parallel with the move to Windows 11, it rolled out to about 20 sites and about 3,000 people, strengthening security and improving operational efficiency at the same time.

### Old-Fashioned Attitudes in a Company with a Long Japanese History
Since 2019, ADK Holdings Inc. (ADK Group) has operated under a four-company holding company structure, as a group that brings together professional units in marketing, creative and content businesses.

In Japan, it covers the whole country with its Tokyo headquarters, seven branch offices and four offices, forming the best team across the boundaries of department, job type and site to help solve its clients' challenges.

But ADK's IT strategy reflected "the typical thinking of a company dependent on SIers about IT systems." Takashi Shibasaki, Group Executive Officer and CIO of ADK Holdings, explains: "Advertising and marketing is a B2B business, but we tend to deliver services flexibly to fit each client's needs, and as a result our dependence on internal systems is low. Except in some departments, the idea of 'transforming business with IT' had trouble taking root."

Still, with the recent trends toward digitalization and stronger governance, momentum grew inside the company to review its IT infrastructure and strengthen its security posture. A major trigger was the ransomware damage at various large companies in Japan.

These security incidents were not someone else's problem for ADK Group. It handles highly confidential information such as clients' new product and campaign information, and it has an animation production company within the group. It therefore decided to advance its security measures at the same time as modernizing the entire IT infrastructure. Makoto Okuyama, General Manager of the IT Platform Department in the Group IT Division, recalls the challenges at the time:

"Our previous IT infrastructure connected to the corporate network at headquarters through firewalls installed at each site. So as new sites opened and companies were integrated through M&A, new firewalls had to be installed, and having firewall settings spread across sites was also a factor that raised security risk."

The shift to full remote work during COVID-19 then exposed the limits of the existing infrastructure. Every employee began to use video conferencing systems such as Teams, and traffic far exceeded expectations. For example, in a meeting of 10 people, when one is absent the other nine all use video, and combined with connections through the VPN the network load rose sharply. Yet increasing bandwidth was inefficient given the balance of cost and headcount.

### Rolling Out to About 20 Sites and 3,000 Users in Three Months
ADK adopted Zscaler to solve these problems. The deciding factors were Zscaler's licensing model and support structure, and the usability of centralized, cloud-based management. With a little over 3,000 employees and one PC per person, a per-site or usage-based charging model would have sent costs soaring.

Mr. Shibasaki says: "We spent about three months on the PoC (proof of concept). During that time, Zscaler's staff carefully explained the benefits and the operating costs in ADK's environment. The pre-deployment support was thorough, and the quick response of their engineers was a big plus." Mr. Okuyama adds: "Technically, we liked the performance gain from connecting directly to large internet exchange points (IX), the affinity with Microsoft Azure and AWS, and cloud-based central management. Until then it was hard to make judgments during incidents because we had no logs. With Zscaler, of course, we can get logs, and they're easy to read, which we appreciated."

The deployment project was effectively completed in 10 months. The process started with preliminary verification in the IT department, went through a pilot of about 200 devices and then moved to full rollout. ADK put a priority on the internal user experience and replaced PCs with Windows 11 alongside the Zscaler rollout, which was a tailwind. Rollout to about 20 sites and 3,000 users was completed in the short span of three months, July to September 2024.

### Stronger Security and Better Management Efficiency at the Same Time
Zscaler greatly improved work efficiency. Keisuke Sue of the Security Management Group in the Information Security Department says: "When working on-site at client locations, people now have the same working environment as in the office. Easy access through single sign-on removed the complex authentication steps required when connecting to the VPN. User convenience has improved dramatically, and the reception has been very good."

The speed of site rollout also increased greatly. For example, adding a BPO (Business Process Outsourcing) center, which previously took three to six months to install a dedicated line, can now be done in one month. Mr. Shibasaki explains: "System integration is smooth even when we do M&A or set up a joint venture."

The simpler network configuration also makes troubleshooting easier. According to Mr. Okuyama, the help desk's handling time for remote connection issues has fallen to about half of what it was before deployment.

Greater visibility into user behavior and finer security controls are further benefits. Mr. Sue explains:

"We can now set fine-grained policies, such as allowing only downloads on file sharing sites, and identify and manage unauthorized SaaS. This lets both the IT Platform Department and the Information Security Department focus on their routine tasks."

### Looking Toward a Cloud-Native Environment
Going forward, ADK plans to use the knowledge gained from deployment to accelerate the reform of its business systems. Specifically, it will move to serverless and containers and build its own development environment, aiming to integrate with its digital foundation for the business.

For IT staff considering Zscaler, Mr. Shibasaki advises "first clarify your own company's challenges," and adds:

"Allow enough time for verification, identify your traffic and take stock of existing applications. Make a plan that puts user experience first. And above all, it is important to change how you think. Move away from the conventional idea of 'protecting sites' and turn toward using a cloud-native environment. That is not just an infrastructure change; it leads to balancing security and usability and to greater business agility."
