## Executive Summary
### Challenges
- The UC system president issued a mandate requiring segmentation of university and visitor business and vulnerability management across university-owned devices.
- Legacy approaches that tied segmentation to location and historical ownership could not scale to UCSB’s 400+ locations and 125,000 devices—many of them BYOD and not centrally managed.
- VPN-based remote access and a patchwork of network stacks and philosophies increased complexity and limited UCSB’s ability to securely support BYOD.

### Phased Journey
- Centralized core network operations and defined a new target-state architecture that replaces VPN and enables policy-based security at scale.
- Implemented agentless device segmentation to discover devices and control east-west traffic.
- Adopted a zero trust approach to application connectivity, meaning users can reach private apps and servers without extending UCSB’s network to unmanaged devices.

### Results & Impact
- Reduces complexity and avoids costs by minimizing the need to build and maintain thousands of traditional network segments and intermediary controls.
- Improves security posture by shrinking the attack surface and limiting lateral movement between university business systems, visitors, and BYOD.
- Accelerates progress toward UC system mandates by enabling consistent, policy-based segmentation and secure access across a highly distributed campus.
